Module 04: Ideation

Divergent Thinking

Going beyond the first obvious solution. Moving from 'what is' to 'what could be'.

01: The Philosophy

Divergent vs Convergent

Ideation is not about finding the 'right' idea immediately. It's about maximizing options before filtering.

DIVERGENT
Create Choices
→
CONVERGENT
Make Choices
Teaching Tip: Remind students that judgment kills ideation. During divergent exercises, there are absolutely no bad ideas. Encourage wild, unconstrained thinking first.

Lateral & Reversal Methods

Sometimes the fastest route to a good idea is to deliberately think in the wrong direction.

The Worst Idea Method

05: Convergent Thinking

From Many Ideas to the Right One

Divergence fills the room with options. Convergence is the discipline of choosing wisely — filtering ideas through human, technical, and business lenses.

🎯

MoSCoW Matrix

Must Have, Should Have, Could Have, Won't Have. Agree on what to build before committing resources.

🔺

Desirability, Feasibility & Viability

IDEO's three lenses — does anyone want it, can we build it, and should we?

📊

Value vs Effort Matrix

A 2×2 grid that plots ideas by value against effort to surface quick wins and strategic bets.

Moodboards

A physical or digital collage of ideas that's used to evoke a specific style or concept.

  • Purpose: Align stakeholders on aesthetic and emotional direction before costly design work begins.
  • Contains: Colors, typography, textures, photography styles, metaphors.
  • Best For: UI/UX design, physical product aesthetics, and branding.

Mindmapping

A diagram used to visually organize information into a hierarchy, showing relationships among pieces of the whole.

  • Core: Write the main problem or core concept in the center.
  • Branches: Create primary themes radiating outward.
  • Twigs: Add specific ideas and details to the branches.

Mindmaps leverage our brain's natural tendency to associate concepts rather than processing them linearly.

Method 6-3-5 (Brainwriting)

A structured method to generate a large number of ideas silently, preventing loud voices from dominating.

1. 6 participants sit in a circle.

2. Each gets a worksheet and writes down 3 ideas in 5 minutes.

3. After 5 minutes, pass the sheet to the person on the right.

4. Read the ideas and build on them or add 3 new ones.

5. Repeat for 6 rounds.

Result: 108 ideas in 30 minutes.

SCAMPER Framework

A checklist of questions to help you innovate on an existing product or service.

Substitute
What materials or rules can be swapped?
Combine
What ideas or features can be merged?
Adapt
What else is like this? How can we copy it?
Modify
Can we change the scale, shape, or color?
Put to another use
Who else could use this? In what context?
Eliminate
What can be removed to simplify it?
Rearrange / Reverse
What happens if we reverse the process or order?

MoSCoW Prioritisation

A convergent technique used to agree on what to build after divergent ideation. Every idea is placed in one of four buckets.

M — Must Have
Non-negotiable. Launch fails without it.
S — Should Have
Important but not vital. Painful to omit.
C — Could Have
Nice-to-have. First to cut when time is short.
W — Won't Have (now)
Explicitly deferred. Prevents scope creep.

The Worst Idea Method

A lateral thinking technique that lowers the barrier to participation and uncovers psychological constraints.

Step 1: Ideate the worst possible ways to solve the problem. Encourage absurd, illegal, or destructive ideas.
(e.g., "To reduce wait times, we just yell at the customers until they leave.")

Step 2: Analyze why it's bad. What exact rule did it break? What core value did it violate?

Step 3: Reverse the worst idea to find a radical good idea.

Desirability, Feasibility & Viability

IDEO's three-lens framework for evaluating whether an idea is worth pursuing. The best ideas sit at the intersection of all three.

DESIRABILITY
Do humans want it?
FEASIBILITY
Can we build it?
VIABILITY
Should we build it?
✨ Sweet
Spot
🧡 Desirability
Is there a real human need? Does this solve a genuine pain point people actually care about?
💙 Feasibility
Is it technically possible today? Do we have (or can we build) the skills, technology, and resources?
💚 Viability
Is there a sustainable business model? Can we make it work financially and operationally long-term?
Teaching Tip: Ask students to plot their ideas on the three-circle Venn diagram. Ideas outside the sweet spot need to be evolved, not abandoned.

Value vs Effort Matrix

A 2×2 prioritisation grid. Plot each idea by its business/user value (Y-axis) against the effort/cost to build (X-axis).

⬆️ HIGH VALUE / LOW EFFORT
🏆 Quick Wins
Do these first. Maximum return for minimum investment.
⬆️ HIGH VALUE / HIGH EFFORT
🎯 Strategic Bets
Plan carefully. Worth it, but needs resources and phasing.
⬇️ LOW VALUE / LOW EFFORT
🔧 Fill-ins
Do if time allows. Not a priority — easy but minor impact.
⬇️ LOW VALUE / HIGH EFFORT
🚫 Time Sinks
Avoid or eliminate. High cost with little return.
How to use it: Write each idea on a sticky note. As a team, debate and place each idea in the correct quadrant. Use the result to sequence your sprint or roadmap.
Teaching Tip: The debate while placing the stickies is more valuable than the final grid. Push students to justify their placement with evidence.
