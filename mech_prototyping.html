Circular to Reciprocating

MANUAL SCRUB

The Slider-Crank mechanism converts continuous rotational motion into linear, back-and-forth (reciprocating) motion.

How it works:

  • Driving Gear / Crank: Rotates in a continuous circle.
  • Connecting Rod: Links the rotating crank to the slider. As the crank turns, it pushes and pulls the rod.
  • Slider: Constrained within a channel, it is forced to move in a straight line back and forth.

Examples: Car engines (pistons), manual water pumps, automated saws.

Circular to Oscillation & Gait

MANUAL SCRUB

A Four-Bar Linkage can be configured to convert circular motion into a sweeping, oscillating motion, or even a complex walking gait.

How it works:

  • Driving Crank: Makes a full continuous rotation.
  • Coupler: Connects the crank to the rocker, transmitting the force.
  • Rocker: Because of its pivot and length, it cannot complete a full circle, so it swings (oscillates) back and forth.

Examples: Windshield wipers, oil pump jacks, kinetic walking robots.

Quick Return Mechanism

MANUAL SCRUB

The Crank and Slotted Lever mechanism creates a stroke that moves slowly forward (for cutting or doing work) and rapidly backwards (to reset efficiently).

How it works:

  • Driving Crank: A small wheel that rotates at a constant speed, sliding a pin inside a lever.
  • Slotted Lever: Pivots at the bottom. It takes more than half a rotation of the crank to push the lever forward, and less than half to pull it back.
  • Ram: Attached to the top of the lever, it performs the linear work.

Examples: Shaper machines, automated assembly arms, specialized cutting tools.

Circular to Linear

MANUAL SCRUB

The Rack and Pinion mechanism provides precise conversion of rotational motion into direct linear translation along a fixed axis.

How it works:

  • Driving Gear (Pinion): A standard circular gear that rotates.
  • Rack: A flat bar with teeth cut into it. As the pinion turns, its teeth engage the rack, driving it in a straight line.

Examples: Car steering systems, CNC machine axes, sliding gates.

Timing by Cams

MANUAL SCRUB

Cam and Follower mechanisms act as physical memory. The shape of the cam physically programs exactly when and how far the follower will move.

How it works:

  • Driving Cam: A non-circular (often teardrop or egg-shaped) piece of metal that rotates on an axis.
  • Follower: A rod or lever that rests against the edge of the cam. As the "lobe" (the bump) of the cam comes around, it pushes the follower up.

Examples: Internal combustion engine valves, automated sewing machines, automata.
