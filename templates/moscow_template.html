MoSCoW Prioritisation Matrix

Design Thinking — Convergent Ideation & Prioritisation Phase

MoSCoW is a convergent prioritisation method. After generating many ideas (e.g., through SCAMPER or 6-3-5), use this matrix to agree on what to build, when. Every idea should land in exactly one quadrant.
The MoSCoW Matrix
M

Must Have

Non-negotiable — ship without this and the product fails

These are the minimum viable requirements. If any Must Have is not delivered, the release is considered a failure. Ask: "Would we cancel launch without this?"

S

Should Have

Important but not vital — painful to omit, not fatal

High-value items that are expected but can be worked around for this iteration. They should be included if time and budget allow. Ask: "Will users be significantly dissatisfied without this?"

C

Could Have

Nice-to-have — small positive impact if included

"Delighters" — desirable but not necessary. Included only when resources remain after Must and Should items are completed. The first to be cut when time is tight. Ask: "Would most users notice if this was missing?"

W

Won't Have (this time)

Out of scope now — agreed to defer, not abandon

Explicitly out of scope for this release. Recording these prevents scope creep and sets stakeholder expectations clearly. These may become Must Haves in a future iteration. Ask: "Can we revisit this next sprint?"
